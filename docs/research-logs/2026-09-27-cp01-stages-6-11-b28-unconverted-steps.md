| Q67 — how Spec 00 admits a step kept in prose, how tools detect it, how its reviewed text is protected, and when it is converted: **contradictory requirement, resolved in Spec 00 v6** | *Conflict.* Spec 00 §2 replaced every step's prose with a contract, and T2 reviews "the converted contract"; the owner decided that Steps 22–31 keep their prose form. Review 5330975497 found that the decision lived only in notes and that a checker enumerating existing contract files cannot notice ten missing contracts. Its required correction offered two options: "either restore and update CP01-S22–S31 plus their verbatim crosswalk mappings, or first amend Spec 00 as the owning specification to define an executable prose-contract form, then update the schema/checker/harness contract and traceability accordingly." The owner's decision excludes the first. *Resolution: the second option, with a convert-before-run form rather than an executable one.* Spec 00 v6 §2 defines an **unconverted step**: its prose is its definition, and the shared requirements and owning specifications apply to it as to a contract. It is listed in `checkpoint.yml` under `prose_steps` with the SHA-256 of its reviewed text: its section and, for the first step of a stage, the stage heading and introduction, which for Stage 10 holds the Kakeibo write authority. A change to that text is an amendment that needs review and records a new hash. Each step is converted or listed, never both and never neither, and has one heading. *Detection.* The checker reports a step heading with neither form, a listed step that also has a contract or has no heading, a repeated heading, and a listed step whose text no longer has its hash. *Execution.* §4: the harness dispatches only converted steps; an unconverted step is eligible once every earlier step is accepted, and the run then pauses as unaccepted and resumable until it is converted. The conversion quotes the reviewed prose verbatim in the crosswalk, adds no obligation and is reviewed; its crosswalk source carries the recorded hash as `reviewed`, and the checker confirms that the replaced prose is the reviewed prose. §5: T1 converts every step except the listed ones; T2 reviews unconverted steps in place and records their hashes; T3 reads `prose_steps` and pauses; T4 tests that pause; T5 converts each unconverted step before running it. This matches the Task 3 plan §2 rule that unconverted Markdown cannot execute or count towards completion. *Why not an executable prose form.* To run prose, a harness would need requirement and criterion IDs, cases and verifier bindings. It would have to extract them from the prose at dispatch, which is the conversion done without review and without a crosswalk. Converting before the run keeps that work reviewed and traceable, and the hash ensures that it starts from the text T2 accepted. Until T5 converts Steps 22–31, their obligations are traced by the T2 review in B22–B27 and pinned by hash, not by IDs. This departs from the review's wording "executable prose-contract form" and needs the owner's confirmation on the PR. *Hashes.* Taken from the text at `ac08ec9`, the head that review 5330975497 examined; that review stated that O1–O5 are resolved and raised only this finding. The text at this commit is identical. Rejected: converting Steps 22–31 now (contradicts the owner's decision); pinning a Git revision instead of a hash (`refactor/pactwright-v2` merges pull requests by squash, so a branch commit such as `ac08ec9` becomes unreachable after merge). |
